Volume 1 — Research, Strategy & Product Discovery

Version: 1.0
Status: Foundation Research Document
Priority: Critical (Must be completed before any design work)

1.0 Purpose

The purpose of this volume is to eliminate assumptions.

Before designing a single screen or writing a single line of code, we must understand:

What already exists.
What works.
What fails.
What makes a portfolio memorable.
What recruiters actually look for.
How premium websites create emotional impact.
Where MS Portfolio will differentiate itself.

This volume transforms inspiration into strategy.

Rule: Never copy. Always understand, extract principles, and improve.

1.1 Research Philosophy

Research is not collecting beautiful websites.

Research is identifying patterns.

For every reference we ask:

Why does this work?
Why does this feel premium?
Why does this create trust?
Why does this keep people scrolling?
Why does this communicate expertise?

Only after answering "why" do we decide whether it belongs in our portfolio.

1.2 Research Objectives

The research phase has six objectives:

Objective 1

Understand the current standard of world-class portfolios.

Objective 2

Understand recruiter psychology.

Objective 3

Objective 4

1.6 Inspiration Extraction Matrix

For every reference, classify elements into:

Reuse

Can be adapted almost directly.

Inspire

The concept is useful, but implementation should be original.

Improve

The idea is good, but we can execute it better.

Reject

Looks attractive but doesn't fit our vision.

1.25 Acceptance Criteria

This volume is considered complete only when:

Every primary competitor has been analyzed using the standard evaluation framework.
User personas are documented.
Recruiter decision flow is understood.
A differentiation strategy has been defined.
Technology choices have been evaluated and justified.
Motion, UX, accessibility, performance, and SEO research have been completed.
A research repository has been created and organized.
1.26 Definition of Done (Volume 1)

Volume 1 is complete when the team can answer, with evidence rather than opinion:

Why this portfolio should exist.
Why each major design decision has been made.
Why each technology has been selected.
Why the information architecture is structured the way it is.
Why the portfolio will stand out in a competitive hiring market.
